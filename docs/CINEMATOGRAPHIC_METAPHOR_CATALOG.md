# Cinematographic metaphor catalog

This is a working vocabulary for translating story and musical behavior into
camera, staging, editing, and vector motion. A device is not a universal symbol;
its meaning comes from context. The `meaning` column is therefore an intended
project reading, not a claim that every audience will interpret the device in
exactly the same way.

Use the stable IDs in scene, transition, choreography, or note blocks. Combine
one dominant spatial metaphor with one performance metaphor and one sonic
material metaphor before adding more. The point is to make revisions
addressable, not to decorate every shot with the whole catalog.

## Camera and spatial relation

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CAM-APPROACH` | Accelerating push-in / dolly-in | Commitment, recognition, being drawn into an idea | Foreground parallax accelerates; subject acquires scale faster than the background | Phrase attack, rising vocal energy | A uniform digital zoom feels like magnification, not travel |
| `CAM-WITHDRAWAL` | Pull-back from detail to world | Isolation, consequence, revelation of context | Begin with one mark; disclose simultaneous action and surrounding scale | Phrase release, structural downbeat | If the wider field is empty, the reveal weakens the scene |
| `CAM-BREATH` | Damped push/pull | A performer or world inhaling and speaking | Small forward overshoot on articulation; slower release on breath | Smoothed vocal amplitude | Constant pumping reads as a music visualizer preset |
| `CAM-ORBIT` | Arc around a subject | Fascination, inspection, changing relationship | Relative parallax changes continuously; foreground crosses faster than background | Sustained phrase, bass motion | Orbiting a static centre without counteraction becomes a product spin |
| `CAM-PROCESSION` | Lateral tracking | Passage, parade, accumulating evidence | New elements enter one frame edge as prior elements leave the other | Repeated beats, lyric list | Equal speed and spacing become a conveyor belt |
| `CAM-ASCENT` | Crane/rise through layers | Release, transcendence, disclosure of pattern | Near layers drop away; individual actors become a larger formation | Section lift, cymbal swell | Rising without a compositional transformation is only relocation |
| `CAM-DESCENT` | Plunge toward an aperture | Investigation, danger, entering the thing beneath | Accelerating foreshortening; aperture expands past frame | Bass drop, drum fill | Centre-only plunge reinforces tunnel vision unless edges counter-move |
| `CAM-SPATIAL-RUPTURE` | Dolly zoom / opposed scale change | Reality changing while the subject remains fixed | Subject scale is held while background perspective expands or contracts | Harmonic shock, semantic reversal | Overuse turns every change into melodrama |
| `CAM-ATTENTION-HANDOFF` | Rack-focus analogue | Thought or agency passing between elements | One depth band gains definition while another softens or loses intensity | Call-and-response, instrumental handoff | Blur alone is illegible in line art; pair it with intensity or detail |
| `CAM-SUSTAINED-REFRAME` | Slow pan and zoom across a held shot | Attention travels from one meaningful anchor to another while local choreography continues | Establish an origin, ease across depth and screen space, and arrive on a destination or consequence; keep the path continuous at adjacent shot boundaries | Phrase progress, narrative handoff, long sustain | Aimless drift adds motion without meaning; audio-rate steering fights the action and causes seasickness |
| `CAM-POV-DRIFT` | Handheld or bodily drift | Subjectivity, uncertainty, physical presence | Non-periodic low-amplitude translation and roll with recoveries | Breath, noisy transients | Pure random jitter looks like a tracking error |

## Framing, staging, and offscreen space

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FRAME-CENTRIPETAL` | Inward-leading frame | Convergence, concentration, capture | Curves, gazes, and trajectories point toward an interior event | Unison, cadence | Everything remaining central for too long makes the world feel small |
| `FRAME-CENTRIFUGAL` | Action visibly continues beyond frame | A larger world, excess, things escaping control | Crop major shapes; maintain trajectories before entrance and after exit | Backing parts, continuing ostinato | Tiny edge decorations do not imply offscreen life |
| `FRAME-LENS-CROSSING` | Foreground object passes the lens | Arrival, passage, a camera embedded in the world | Near object expands rapidly, occludes, then exits beyond an edge | Drum attack, camera step | A flat wipe with no parallax feels like a transition overlay |
| `FRAME-NEGATIVE-WAKE` | Empty space gains agency | Absence, potential, unnamed presence | Surrounding lines bend around a void; the void changes silhouette before it is filled | Master density, sustained tone | Drawing a dark blob removes the ambiguity |
| `FRAME-PERIPHERAL-COUNTERPOINT` | Secondary action at edges | A living production rather than a single event | Background actors continue independent arcs, partly offscreen and under occlusion | Backing vocals, percussion subdivisions | Identical loops become wallpaper and compete with the lead |
| `FRAME-SCALE-BILLING` | Importance expressed through size and proximity | Who or what the phrase is about | Lead advances and enlarges on enunciation; recedes or leaves on breath | Damped vocal amplitude and onsets | Scale without position or pose change feels like UI hover feedback |
| `FRAME-PATTERN-SURRENDER` | Individuals form a larger image | Collective identity, system, spectacle | Repeated actors temporarily become ring, spiral, fan, word, or silhouette | Chorus boundary, ensemble hit | Maintaining the pattern too long erases character |
| `FRAME-CHROMATIC-TUNNEL` | Broad color bands become the stage | Immersion, passage, rhythm as architecture | Concentric colored annuli and radial planes establish full-frame depth; foreground actions interrupt them | Percussion body and structural accents | Thin rings behind a small centre remain decoration rather than architecture |

## Editing and association

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EDIT-KULESHOV` | Associative juxtaposition | A neutral element acquires meaning from its neighbor | Place the same face/shape beside different objects or aftermaths | Lyric comparison, call-and-response | Without a readable pair, the audience invents an unintended relation |
| `EDIT-GRAPHIC-MATCH` | Match cut or morph on shared contour | Continuity through transformation | Preserve a curve, axis, or motion while identity changes | Timbre change, scene boundary | Matching only colour produces a weak connection |
| `EDIT-MATERIAL-HANDOFF` | Outgoing matter adopts the incoming world's rules | Extreme contrast that still feels causal | Preserve one contour, object family, or gesture across the boundary while changing its palette, projection, density, motion grammar, and musical parameter mapping | Section change with a shared release/attack or semantic handoff | A generic dissolve changes scenery without explaining why the two worlds belong together |
| `EDIT-RHYTHMIC-COLLISION` | Short contrasting images on accents | Argument, impact, acceleration | Cut or replace dominant scale/direction at selected transients | Drum fills, stabs | Cutting on every beat destroys hierarchy |
| `EDIT-PARALLEL-ACTION` | Intercut simultaneous processes | Causality, comparison, convergence | Maintain distinct visual grammars, then allow one shared event | Two vocalists, answering instruments | Similar-looking locations make the threads impossible to track |
| `EDIT-PHRASE-DISSOLVE` | Phrase-boundary dissolve or superimposition | Memory, continuity, material changing state | Outgoing contour persists while incoming material forms beneath it | Sustained release, reverb tail | A generic crossfade hides rather than transforms |
| `EDIT-CONTEXT-REFRESH` | Major compositional renewal inside one scene | Sustained attention without discarding scene identity | At a lyric, phrase, or structural cue, change at least two of camera angle, shot scale, screen axis, depth composition, dominant entrant, or foreground/background hierarchy while preserving the scene's recognizable material | Lyric turn, new speaker, arrangement handoff, sectional midpoint | Small parameter changes inside the same frame context do not reset visual fatigue; arbitrary changes feel like restless coverage |

### Frame-context pacing rule

Treat roughly eight seconds in one frame context as a review checkpoint and
twelve seconds as the ordinary ceiling without a major introduction or
compositional renewal. This is a pacing heuristic, not an automatic cutting
rule: a deliberately sustained moving hold may exceed it when tension or
stillness is the point. A valid refresh changes at least two structural
dimensions—camera angle, scale, axis, depth composition, dominant subject, or
foreground/background hierarchy—and should land on an identifiable lyric,
phrase, speaker, or arrangement event. Changing only glow, amplitude, object
pose, or local animation does not establish a new frame context.

## Performance and motion

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PERF-ANTICIPATION` | Preparation before action | Intention and weight | Draw back, compress, or briefly reduce scale before the accent | Predicted beat or phrase onset | Anticipating every event makes motion mannered |
| `PERF-OVERLAP` | Follow-through and secondary action | Life, mass, simultaneous thought | Eyes, mouth, hands, foliage, and trailing vectors settle at different rates | Separate attack/release constants | Moving every component together creates a rigid puppet |
| `PERF-ARC` | Curved line of action | Grace, physical coherence | Translate and rotate along S-curves, arcs, and overshooting recoveries | Phrase contour | Slow perfect circles feel mechanical |
| `PERF-EXAGGERATED-ENUNCIATION` | Brief scale and shape amplification | Vocal intent made bodily | Mouth width/opening and performer proximity overshoot strong syllables, then damp | Lead-vocal waveform, viseme, amplitude | Unsmooth audio control becomes frantic chatter |
| `PERF-HYSTERETIC-PRESENCE` | Fast entrance, held presence, slow release | A performer owns a phrase instead of pumping word by word | Rise quickly with the voice, hold across short gaps, decay slowly after the phrase, and let sustained delivery continue accumulating scale | Lead-vocal envelope with asymmetric attack/hold/release and phrase continuity | Symmetric smoothing shrinks the performer between syllables; hard limiting prevents a long phrase from gaining force |
| `PERF-ABSENCE` | Performer leaves on breath | Space as part of the performance | Opacity, scale, and position all retreat; another layer inherits attention | Vocal gate with hysteresis | Leaving only a dim tiny face reads as a rendering bug |
| `PERF-RESPONSE-CHAIN` | Authored entity-to-entity reaction | Agency, personality, social relation without simulated physics | Initiator approaches; responder accommodates or deforms; contact gives each an unequal recoil and follow-through | Phrase beat, lyric verb, selected transient | Generic attraction or collision makes every entity share one personality |

## Visual music and sonic material

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SONIC-WAVEFORM-SKIN` | Actual waveform displaces a contour | The sound physically lives in the object | A band-limited signed waveform travels along lips, ribbons, or an edge | Lead vocal or selected stem at a render-safe high rate | An envelope or arbitrary sine wave cannot preserve the performer’s cycle irregularity |
| `SONIC-LISSAJOUS-BODY` | Large XY/Lissajous vector figure | Phase, harmonic relationship, bass as spatial mass | Two or three oscillatory axes form a closed curve; frequency ratio alters lobes | Bass signal, pitch/centroid, density | A small centred loop reads as a logo; crop it and move it through depth |
| `SONIC-TRAVELLING-RIPPLE` | Ripple moving along an existing vector | Transmission, speech, energy passing between points | Phase advances over arc length; local amplitude follows the stem | Vocal waveform, spectral flux | Uniform wobble makes the whole drawing gelatinous |
| `SONIC-PERSISTENCE-BLUR` | Temporal supersampling reveals vibration band | Energy too fast to see but present as material | Keep a solid restrained core and a narrow high-rate moving edge | 30–280 Hz carrier during high-sample render | Large vibration amplitudes average into fog and surrender the contour |
| `SONIC-PHRASE-COLOR` | Colour changes at musical sentences | Section identity and orchestration made visible | Palette changes at phrase edges; intensity still responds continuously | Phrase/section boundary, dynamics | A literal pitch-to-colour law becomes arbitrary unless the project establishes it |
| `SONIC-DRAWN-TRACK` | Sound data becomes visible striation or column | “See what you hear” rather than decorate the music | Rhythm or waveform is moved directly into picture structure | Percussive pattern, repeated note grid | One permanent equalizer layout becomes informational rather than cinematic |
| `SONIC-NOTE-ACTOR` | Selected MIDI notes become scene-native performers | Exact pitch, duration and velocity enter the story without exposing a piano roll | Note-on controls arrival/contact, duration elongates the form, pitch changes trajectory or colour, and velocity changes bloom; show only selected phrases | Exact DAW MIDI notes on the tempo-ramp clock | Continuous note rain becomes a screensaver and competes with the featured performer |
| `SONIC-CHORD-FORMATION` | Simultaneous MIDI notes perform as one connected ensemble | Harmony becomes a spatial body while every pitch stays readable | Preserve individual pitch height, hue, duration and velocity, but connect shared-onset notes with a harness, line of action, or group silhouette; interval span controls formation width and the strongest velocity may lead its depth | Chord onset, sustained voicing, harmonic change | Independent particles hide simultaneity; collapsing a chord into one blob erases voicing and register |
| `SONIC-MASS-FOLLOWER` | Slow musical body controls visual weight | Sound gains material weight without flicker | Beat/phrase followers drive stroke thickness, glow, color area, and apparent mass; waveform remains a fine edge deformation | Smoothed RMS, articulation, phrase envelope | Audio-rate thickness and glow look unstable even when the geometry is correct |

## Generative and environmental transformation

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GROW-GENERATION-CASCADE` | Discrete recursive generation plus continuous growth | Perpetual becoming, ideas breeding ideas | New child branches grow while the prior generation shifts down/out and is absorbed | Beat count chooses generation; envelope controls interpolation | One monotonic reveal reaches a static finished plant |
| `GROW-PROPAGATION-WAVE` | Growth event travels through the scene | Causality, communication, contagion | A transient activates near roots, then progressively farther stems | Drum transient and event age | Simultaneous activation reads as global pulsing |
| `GROW-REPLACEMENT` | New form physically displaces old form | Renewal, pressure, succession | Incoming generation pushes the older silhouette toward an edge or lower depth | Repeated phrase, ostinato | Crossfading in place loses the sense of force |
| `GROW-SELF-SIMILAR-ZOOM` | Camera crosses levels of a fractal-like form | Scale without a stable floor | A branch resembles the whole plant; the camera enters a child and discovers another system | Section transition, bass descent | Exact repetition becomes a screensaver; vary curvature and timing |
| `GROW-ENVIRONMENT-ANSWER` | Scene responds in different places to different instruments | Ensemble inhabiting one world | Drums trigger growth events, bass deforms large roots, vocals articulate flowers or faces | DAW-aligned stems | Mapping every stem to brightness collapses the ensemble back into one parameter |

## Classic hand-drawn animation grammar

These devices translate traditional character-animation practice into the
project's line, polygon, camera, and environment vocabulary. Several overlap
with the performance section; use the `ANIM-` IDs when the drawing or timing
technique itself is the intended note.

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ANIM-KEY-POSE` | Pose-to-pose key drawing | A phrase has readable authored intentions rather than continuous wandering | Define the anticipation, contact, recoil, and final attitude first; interpolate only after the silhouettes read | Lyric verb, phrase boundary | Smooth interpolation between weak poses remains weak |
| `ANIM-BIOLOGICAL-RETARGET` | Captured organic motion drives a nonliteral drawn form | Weight, intent and counter-motion survive radical visual abstraction | Keep a hidden reduced landmark rig; map its joints to selected Bézier controls, detached features or extremities while scene paths and styling remain authored | Performance phrase, gait or gesture cycle, selected musical anchors | Drawing the skeleton literally looks clinical; smoothing every irregularity removes the living evidence the capture was meant to supply |
| `ANIM-ANTICIPATION` | Contrary preparation | An entity intends its next action | Briefly compress, lean, look, or travel opposite the coming move | Predicted onset, pickup | Equal anticipation before every beat becomes a tic |
| `ANIM-SQUASH-STRETCH` | Volume-preserving deformation | Weight, elasticity, impact, breath | Compress one axis while expanding another; restore on a slower curve | Impact and release | Uniform scaling reads as zoom rather than material response |
| `ANIM-SLOW-IN-OUT` | Uneven inbetween spacing | Mass and intentional timing | Cluster temporal samples around key poses and move quickly through the middle | Phrase attack/release | One easing curve on every object erases personality |
| `ANIM-ARC` | Curved trajectory and line of action | Grace, anatomy, coherent force | Primary motion and secondary extremities follow related but unequal arcs | Phrase contour | Perfect circles become mechanical or ornamental |
| `ANIM-FOLLOW-THROUGH` | Parts settle after the main mass | Momentum, softness, layered thought | Lead mass stops first; leaves, mouths, tendrils, glow and trails overshoot and return at separate rates | Release, reverb tail | Delaying the whole drawing together is merely latency |
| `ANIM-SECONDARY-ACTION` | Supporting gesture | Character and context without stealing the beat | A gaze, leaf curl, color ripple, or peripheral actor reinforces the main action | Backing part, subdivision | A louder secondary action destroys staging hierarchy |
| `ANIM-SMEAR` | One drawn form spans a fast transition | Speed perceived as one authored gesture rather than strobing positions | Stretch, duplicate or simplify the contour along its motion arc for a short interval | Fast accent, whip transition | Persistent smear looks like a malformed resting design |
| `ANIM-MOVING-HOLD` | Held pose with internal life | Attention, suspense, thought | Hold the dominant silhouette while line boil, breath, gaze or one extremity continues | Sustained note, rest | Freezing every channel makes the renderer look stopped |
| `ANIM-LINE-BOIL` | Small redraw variation | Handmade energy, instability, living material | Slowly perturb control points or contour phase without changing the pose | Low-rate noise, timbral flux | High-rate random jitter reads as poor tracking |
| `ANIM-MORPH` | Identity changes through shared topology | Association, dream logic, transformation | Preserve one recognizable contour or motion while internal landmarks become the next entity | Timbre handoff, match transition | Dissolving unrelated drawings is not a morph |
| `ANIM-IMPACT-ACCENT` | Extreme contact drawing | Force and punctuation | Brief silhouette inversion, starburst, flattened form, color field or one-frame construction exposure | Selected transient | Accenting every hit removes emphasis |
| `ANIM-MULTIPLANE` | Layers move at different rates | Spatial depth in a drawn world | Separate foreground, performance, stage, and distant marks; vary parallax, scale rate and occlusion | Camera travel | Scaling a single flattened composition cannot produce real parallax |
| `ANIM-CYCLE-VARIATION` | Repeated action with altered path or timing | Continuity without mechanical looping | Reuse a rhythmic cycle but change entry edge, arc, scale, phase or responder | Ostinato, repeated lyric | Exact repetition exposes the loop immediately |
| `ANIM-SILHOUETTE-STAGING` | Action readable as a flat shape | Clarity before surface detail | Separate limbs, features, negative spaces and directional curves in the dominant pose | Structural beat | Glow and detail cannot rescue an ambiguous silhouette |
| `ANIM-OFFSCREEN-CONTINUATION` | A form fades or curves beyond its visible source | A larger action or organism continues outside the frame | Taper and fade a stem, trail or procession while its tangent continues toward depth or an edge | Sustained growth, procession | A hard terminal point makes the form look clipped or spawned from nowhere |

## Vector-compatible depth cues

Avoid depending on atmospheric desaturation when it weakens the laser/vector
language. Combine several of these cues; no single one needs to carry depth by
itself.

| ID | Device | Intended meaning | Screen mechanics | Useful musical driver | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DEPTH-OCCLUSION` | Near contours interrupt farther layers | Ordinal front/back placement | Give near shapes a dark knockout or solid underlay before drawing their luminous core | Camera proximity | Screen-additive strokes that never hide one another collapse into one plane |
| `DEPTH-DYNAMIC-OCCLUSION` | Background is progressively revealed and concealed | Camera or object motion through space | Let a moving near edge uncover new background marks on one side while covering them on the other | Camera travel, actor crossing | A global crossfade supplies no spatial ordering |
| `DEPTH-MOTION-PARALLAX` | Near layers move faster than far layers | Viewer travel and layer separation | Use separate world depths and unequal screen velocity during dolly, pan or orbit | Camera path | Merely delaying identical paths looks like echo rather than depth |
| `DEPTH-RELATIVE-SCALE` | Comparable motifs have different size | Distance and billing | Repeat a recognizable mark at distinct projected scales | Stable scene motif | Unrelated shapes provide no reliable size comparison |
| `DEPTH-SCALE-VELOCITY` | Near objects expand faster during approach | Passage toward the lens | Increase apparent scale rate nonlinearly as camera distance closes | Dolly progress | Uniform zoom makes every depth layer advance together |
| `DEPTH-CONTOUR-WEIGHT` | Near geometry carries more line mass | Proximity without haze | Increase stroke width and solid core with projected scale, using slow followers | Projected depth, phrase body | Audio-rate thickness flickers and confuses depth with loudness |
| `DEPTH-GLOW-ENVELOPE` | Bloom expands toward the lens | Near/far separation in luminous line art | Couple glow radius to depth more strongly than brightness | Projected depth | Equal glow everywhere turns the scene into luminous fog |
| `DEPTH-DETAIL-GRADIENT` | Recursive detail increases with proximity | Scale and focus hierarchy | Reveal child branches, interior marks or articulation only when large enough | Projected size | Tiny distant detail aliases and flattens the image |
| `DEPTH-EDGE-CROSSING` | A near form continues beyond the image | Camera embedded in a larger world | Crop large silhouettes and preserve their tangent before entrance and after exit | Dolly, orbit | Small complete objects floating inside frame feel stage-bound |
| `DEPTH-FORESHORTENED-TAIL` | A contour bends toward or away from camera | Orientation through depth | Change width, glow, curvature and spacing along a fading continuation tail | Growth direction | A uniformly tapered tail reads as a decorative flourish |
| `DEPTH-COLOR-ORDER` | Depth layers keep distinct color roles | Separation without desaturation | Reserve related hues or luminance bands for far, middle and near structures while retaining saturation | Scene palette | Random per-object color destroys the ordering cue |

## Current opening selection

The opening uses camera motion as authored attention, not ambient drift:

- `CAM-APPROACH` makes the first signal aperture and later speaker close-ups
  feel like travel through depth rather than uniform digital magnification;
- `CAM-ASCENT` discloses the Berkeley fan as a larger formation, while
  `CAM-ORBIT` changes the relationship between the tangible pieces and their
  near-fit seams;
- `CAM-ATTENTION-HANDOFF` transfers agency between Them 1, Them 2, travelling
  note parcels, and the consequence of each failed fit;
- `CAM-SUSTAINED-REFRAME` must begin on one readable anchor and arrive on
  another. A pan, push, or roll that does not reveal, compare, transfer, or
  cause something is removed;
- `EDIT-CONTEXT-REFRESH` lands at lyric, phrase, or arrangement turns before
  the opening remains in one frame context long enough to lose attention.

No opening environment is a static backdrop. Every shot keeps at least two
independent depth systems moving at unequal speeds, phases, scales, and arcs.
The available systems are cropped drum rings, bass horizons, exact-note
bodies, backing-vocal echoes, rust planetary striations, bowed river currents,
recursive leaf veins, offscreen fragments, and star or dust motes. Their
hierarchy follows `FRAME-PERIPHERAL-COUNTERPOINT`: they reinforce the featured
action and continue beyond the frame, but do not become synchronized wallpaper
or compete for billing.

Use `ANIM-MULTIPLANE`, `DEPTH-MOTION-PARALLAX`,
`DEPTH-DYNAMIC-OCCLUSION`, and `DEPTH-EDGE-CROSSING` so that the environmental
motion exposes camera travel and spatial ordering. Performers and major shapes
may hold, but only as `ANIM-MOVING-HOLD`: gaze, breath, line boil, trailing
contours, and peripheral actors settle at unequal rates.

A void is a deliberate narrative device, never the absence of rendering. Use
it only for anticipation, a missing fit, an attention handoff, or failed
delivery. Under `FRAME-NEGATIVE-WAKE`, surrounding contours bend around the
empty region while sparse star or dust particles continue through several
depths with `ANIM-CYCLE-VARIATION`. Keep the void's centre visually quiet so
its absence remains readable.

## Current garden selection

This revision deliberately uses a bounded subset:

- `FRAME-CENTRIFUGAL` and `FRAME-LENS-CROSSING` for large swirls and foliage
  that continue beyond the frame;
- `FRAME-SCALE-BILLING`, `PERF-EXAGGERATED-ENUNCIATION`, and `PERF-ABSENCE`
  for the lead singer’s approach, beat-rock, mouth articulation, and breath;
- `SONIC-WAVEFORM-SKIN` on the lips and `SONIC-LISSAJOUS-BODY` on the bass;
- `SONIC-PERSISTENCE-BLUR` with a restrained solid core for high-sample output;
- `GROW-GENERATION-CASCADE` and `GROW-REPLACEMENT` for plants that never
  settle into a final state;
- `FRAME-PERIPHERAL-COUNTERPOINT` for independent background and offstage life.
- `FRAME-CHROMATIC-TUNNEL` and `SONIC-MASS-FOLLOWER` make the percussion a
  full-frame color architecture whose visual weight changes more slowly than
  the waveform texture;
- `PERF-RESPONSE-CHAIN` structures the authored approach, contact, recoil, and
  follow-through between addressable entities.
- `ANIM-OFFSCREEN-CONTINUATION`, `DEPTH-FORESHORTENED-TAIL`, and
  `DEPTH-OCCLUSION` keep plant roots from appearing at a cut endpoint: their
  source curves away, tapers, fades, and can knock out more distant color.
- `ANIM-MULTIPLANE`, `DEPTH-MOTION-PARALLAX`, `DEPTH-CONTOUR-WEIGHT`,
  `DEPTH-GLOW-ENVELOPE`, and `DEPTH-DETAIL-GRADIENT` reinforce depth without
  washing out the distant vector layers.

## Reference basis

- [Yale National Initiative: framing and film movement](https://teachers.yale.edu/curriculum/units/2006/1/4/4)
  relates shot scale, camera angle, tracking, panning, and handheld movement to
  intimacy, context, point of view, and audience response.
- [Yale National Initiative: centripetal and centrifugal framing](https://teachers.yale.edu/curriculum/units/2022/1/2/5)
  distinguishes inward-enclosing frames from frames that deliberately lead the
  eye offscreen.
- [StudioBinder camera movement guide](https://www.studiobinder.com/camera-shots/camera-movements/)
  is a practical catalogue of pans, tilts, dollies, zooms, tracking, arcs, and
  the dolly zoom.
- [Academy film-editing activities guide](https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/complet_film_editing_activities_guide.pdf)
  describes editing as writing with images and explains associative context
  through the Kuleshov experiment.
- [Disney Animation process](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/)
  names timing, staging, anticipation, follow-through, and secondary action as
  foundations of character animation.
- [Walt Disney Family Museum: Multiplane Camera curriculum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/MultiplaneGuideCurriculumPacket_Final.pdf)
  describes the layered camera system used to create parallax and convincing
  depth in drawn animation.
- [OpenStax Psychology: Vision](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/5-3-vision)
  summarizes monocular depth cues including linear perspective, interposition,
  relative size, and position relative to the horizon.
- [McGill Vision Research: dynamic occlusion and motion parallax](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4521857/)
  distinguishes expansion/compression from the accretion and deletion of
  visible background information at moving occlusion boundaries.
- [NFB: *Synchromy*](https://www.nfb.ca/film/synchromy/) and
  [McLaren's technical notes](https://www3.nfb.ca/archives_mclaren/notech/NT29EN.pdf)
  provide a direct visual-music precedent: soundtrack striations moved into the
  picture, separately choreographed musical parts, and colour changes at
  musical sentences.
- [Tektronix XY display guide](https://www.tek.com/en/support/faqs/how-do-i-utilize-xy-display-feature-dpo-mso-mdo4000-series-oscilloscope)
  explains how Lissajous shapes expose frequency ratios and phase relationships.
- [MDN Web Audio visualization guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Visualizations_with_Web_Audio_API)
  distinguishes time-domain waveform data from frequency-domain analysis and
  shows the former drawn as an oscilloscope trace.
- [Prusinkiewicz, Hammel, and Mjolsness: Animation of Plant Development](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/animdev.sig93.html)
  combines discrete L-system productions with continuous interpolation for
  time-lapse-like developmental animation.
