Let the Phone Book Sing — visual story direction

User direction
The singer makes the deliberately unpromising act of singing an old phone book entertaining through an excellent performance. As she sings each listing, the film cuts into the life behind that name, number and address: unusual people doing unusual things. The result should feel like a rhythmic tour of the whole city hidden in the book.

Style
Carry the illustrated singer reference's elongated, elegant cartoon/graphic-novel proportions through the world and supporting cast, with controlled ink contours, crosshatching and painted material texture. The new project has its own people and settings; do not transplant the Harper/Taffy desert world.

Visible narrator convention
The singer appears within or near the locations while performing toward the audience. She is visible to us, but the residents do not perceive, acknowledge or react to her. Their activities unfold independently of her presence. Her performance and movement should feel natural, rather than a sequence of presentational pointing gestures. Her passage between locations can use cuts and animation's spatial freedom. Treat her as visually solid in the same drawing style; no ghost treatment is requested.

Phone book
The latest user direction uses an open ratty phone book for the cover still, sung from with an expressive musical performance. The user expects to drop it for most of the eventual video because carrying it everywhere is cumbersome. The book is optional for video shots, not a mandatory recurring prop. This supersedes both the original visual exclusion and any assumption that she carries it throughout.

First listing — user developing this resident
Wait, Patience — 1 Pleasant Avenue, upstairs — 109-1009

Why this listing
Patience creates a useful expectation that can be fulfilled or overturned by the resident's activity. The upstairs address gives the scene a clear spatial identity and a potential reveal. Hope Wait lives at the rear of the same building, providing a later connected vignette without requiring that second scene now.

Patience Wait — user direction
Patience is a woman who is impatient and always late, rushing to get ready to go to work. Keep warmth and humor; avoid a stereotyped elderly spinster. The cast should have strongly exaggerated, instantly recognizable silhouettes, with physical traits pushed deliberately rather than making everyone conventionally proportioned. First character proposal: character-development/patience-wait-reference-v001.notes.txt and its paired image/prompt. That proposed appearance still awaits review.

City and neighborhood — user direction
A warm fictional coastal city, combining inspiration from San Francisco and San Diego without literally depicting either. Pleasant Avenue is a nice, design-conscious neighborhood. An attractive low-rise/multi-story building with 1970s-inspired design is a possible direction. Whether this is a divided house, apartments or an upstairs dwelling over a shop is unresolved. This is visual inspiration, not a claim about the cities' actual climate or a locked historical period.

Musical motion — proposed approach, not a completed result
Use the existing DAW-derived grid to place cuts and chosen gesture/action accents. Favor short repeatable actions (a step, turn, tap, lift or object movement) that can be looped or explicitly keyed to beats. Reserve controlled drawn/rigged animation for movements that must match exact musical events. Generated footage can supply broader performance, with selective trimming/retiming where it works; do not promise exact full-body dancing from unconstrained generated footage.
