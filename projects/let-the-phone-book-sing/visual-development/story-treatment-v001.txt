STORY PROPOSAL
She begins with 'nothing to sing' and discovers a city full of subjects. First we see separate household absurdities; then connections between neighbors; finally a community going about its evening. Everyday motions become visual accompaniment through editing, not because residents hear or obey her. Warm observational comedy; each resident has a desire, not just a literal name costume.
The place/name establishes the setup; the sung number gives the action time to land. Do not animate a separate event for every digit or require viewers to solve numerical puzzles.

STRUCTURE
Intro (section 1): narrator on a coastal street stoop, empty-handed performance gesture on 'nothing'; she discovers/opens the battered directory beside her. Move into its first location. Leave the book on the stoop when it becomes cumbersome.
Verse 1 (section 2): home, rooftop, opposite gardens. Establish the city's playful logic.
Chorus 1 (section 3): singer walks the block in a mostly continuous performance shot. Recognizable windows and flowers sit behind her; a couple of everyday movements rhyme with her phrasing.
Verse 2 (section 4): tiny shop, grand house, riverside repair yard, opposite doorstep. Shift to ambition, money and effort.
Chorus 2 (section 5): expand to a crossing and deeper street view. Singer gets larger movement and more room; coin, wheel and garden gestures recur behind her as visual rhythms. Residents remain absorbed in their tasks.
Verse 3 (section 6): courtyard forest, woodworking room, topiary garden, waterside stall. More exaggerated silhouettes and material comedy, with neighbors visibly connected.
Chorus 3 (section 7): singer on a rising stair/terrace; camera reveals several neighborhoods. Familiar actions recur in separate depths, giving the impression of a visual ensemble. Keep her face/performance primary.
Verse 4 (section 8): waiting, rushing, strolling, flowing. Return to familiar geography and resolve the runner setup, with a warmer emotional turn.
Final chorus (section 9): start close on the singer; 'Thin little pages / Whole city inside' opens into a layered view of the neighborhoods already visited. 'I'm singing you' briefly returns to individual faces. 'I never called you / You still got through' lands on her recognition and affection. No telephone call or residents looking at her is needed.
