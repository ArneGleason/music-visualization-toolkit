EXISTING ANCHORS
Harper, Taffy, the water broker and his one shoulder lookout remain the core.
New supporting characters must be readable through silhouette, routine and action
without requiring spoken exposition. A role's motivation is personal, not a
claim about a species or a culture. Keep the main theft dependent on Harper's
attention act and Taffy's timing. The horseshoe key stays with the broker until
Taffy takes it; no candidate silently moves the theft to another target.

MARKET APPROACH

A1 — The Shade Stitcher
Silhouette: Tall human, narrow apron, triangular bundle of shade battens rising over one shoulder.
Setup / motivation: Paid to keep the market's shade intact; another torn panel costs her the afternoon's earnings.
Quirk: Holds spare pegs between her lips and tests every knot with a hard tug.
Choreographic role: Her routine lowering and raising of a cloth panel gives Harper a visible escape opportunity to plan for.
Useful alternate: C2

A2 — The Water Porter
Silhouette: Short, ochre-skinned humanoid beneath a wide yoke of squat ceramic jars.
Setup / motivation: Responsible for delivering a full measure; every lost drop comes out of his pay.
Quirk: Stops to settle the water before making a tight turn.
Choreographic role: Creates a slow moving bottleneck that the children can time and a pursuing guard must negotiate without wrecking valuable stock.

A3 — The Scrap Appraiser
Silhouette: Small older human, tiered pockets, one oversized folding magnifier.
Setup / motivation: Buys useful salvage cheaply and fears paying for a worthless part.
Quirk: Weighs a piece in one palm before ever looking at its seller.
Choreographic role: Takes an interest in what Taffy is carrying; the boy has to conceal the newly stolen device while appearing ordinary.
Useful alternate: C6

A4 — The Screen Keeper
Silhouette: Lanky humanoid carrying a flat triangular ladder and a rolled cleaning pad.
Setup / motivation: Maintains public information displays and needs traders to keep subscribing.
Quirk: Polishes one particular corner even while reading the screen.
Choreographic role: Gives the opening information display a human presence; the pair can observe its update cycle while looking like casual passersby.
Useful alternate: D3

A5 — The Flask Redeemer
Silhouette: Broad human in a low sun hat, pushing a tiny cart piled above their own head.
Setup / motivation: Collects returnable containers and remembers precisely who owes a deposit.
Quirk: Rattles each flask beside an ear to check that it is empty.
Choreographic role: A noisy, awkward crossing can conceal a brief movement, but brushing the cart risks attracting everyone's attention.
Useful alternate: A2

A6 — The Quiet Stallholder
Silhouette: Long-fingered offworld human behind a shallow stall with folding wooden wings.
Setup / motivation: Needs enough sales to pay tomorrow's rent; dislikes the water broker's cut.
Quirk: Keeps straightening a single item customers never buy.
Choreographic role: Notices the children arrive together, then separately; can choose to continue a sale rather than inform the broker. A small decision with moral weight.

THE CRIMINAL'S CREW

B1 — Doorpost
Silhouette: Broad wedge-shaped humanoid, heavy work coat, one shoulder padded for moving crates.
Setup / motivation: Paid to keep people away from the boss and treats personal space as a strict boundary.
Quirk: Turns his whole body instead of just his head.
Choreographic role: His turns open and close the line of sight to Taffy. He can become the visible pursuer Harper draws into the public passage.
Useful alternate: B5

B2 — The Tester
Silhouette: Very thin human in a faded but meticulously kept apron, ceramic inspection lens on a cord.
Setup / motivation: Values jewels for the broker and needs to remain the person who cannot be fooled.
Quirk: Always tries to take an object out of its owner's hand to examine it properly.
Choreographic role: Raises the stakes of Harper's performance by trying to inspect the fake gem. She has to keep control while directing his and the broker's gaze.
Useful alternate: B3

B3 — Tally
Silhouette: Compact older humanoid, rectangular ledger boards folded against the chest.
Setup / motivation: Keeps the broker's accounts and expects to be blamed for missing property.
Quirk: Counts tools and cups as reflexively as money.
Choreographic role: Can notice the empty key holster after the children leave and initiate the alert. Gives the wanted broadcast a possible causal origin.
Useful alternate: B2

B4 — Fast Coat
Silhouette: Long-legged humanoid in a sleeveless coat with two blunt hanging tails.
Setup / motivation: A junior enforcer hoping that a conspicuous success will earn a better position.
Quirk: Signals bystanders out of his way as if he already commands them.
Choreographic role: A fast, committed pursuer makes Harper's decoy route dangerous; she needs concealment and a planned turn to lose him.
Useful alternate: B1

B5 — Stillwater
Silhouette: Ordinary-looking human in an unremarkable plain wrap, small still movements.
Setup / motivation: Earns a living spotting accomplices while louder colleagues confront the obvious distraction.
Quirk: Watches hands and reflections rather than faces.
Choreographic role: Makes the hidden partnership the central risk. The boy needs a real broken sightline; this watcher is difficult to draw toward the gem.
Useful alternate: B1

B6 — Valve Hand
Silhouette: Squat humanoid, oversized padded gloves, short apron marked by mineral deposits.
Setup / motivation: Maintains the broker's pumps; a damaged valve is his problem even when the boss causes it.
Quirk: Moves other people's cups off machinery without asking.
Choreographic role: Can interrupt the jewel exchange to reach a control beside the chair, briefly threatening Taffy's cover without having noticed him.
Useful alternate: C1

SERVICE ROUTES AND REUNION

C1 — The Pipe Fitter
Silhouette: Human with one long protective glove, a shoulder roll of tools and a low work stool.
Setup / motivation: Must finish a pressure adjustment before the supply cycle changes.
Quirk: Answers everything with their eyes still on the pressure needle.
Choreographic role: A valve release masks a few footsteps, while the fitter's work position closes one route. The children must read and time that routine.
Useful alternate: C4

C2 — The Cloth Carrier
Silhouette: A walking rectangular frame draped with several uneven lengths of repaired awning.
Setup / motivation: Returning shade panels to their owners and unwilling to deliver dusty work twice.
Quirk: Snaps each bottom corner straight before folding.
Choreographic role: A moving cloth screen gives Harper a moment to vanish. Strong alternative to using the market stitcher's panel.
Useful alternate: A1

C3 — The Route Runner
Silhouette: Lean older teenager, cropped work jacket, one long dispatch tube strapped horizontally.
Setup / motivation: A late delivery threatens their wages; the service ledge is their usual shortcut.
Quirk: Changes the tube from side to side at every narrow opening without stopping.
Choreographic role: Meets Taffy coming the other way and forces him into a pump recess. That brief interruption can hide him from the street or expose the sack.

C4 — The Pressure Warden
Silhouette: Broad human in a stiff sun cape, carrying a flat inspection board.
Setup / motivation: Responsible for keeping the public out of working water infrastructure.
Quirk: Checks a latch twice, even after seeing it catch.
Choreographic role: Closes the obvious maintenance gate on schedule, forcing a choice of path. Needs an early glimpse of the routine so the obstacle feels earned.
Useful alternate: C1

C5 — The Late Lunch
Silhouette: Heavy-set worker seated beneath a pier, circular food tins stacked in a neat tower.
Setup / motivation: Has finally found ten quiet minutes and has no affection for the broker.
Quirk: Sets every bite on a clean tin lid before dealing with an interruption.
Choreographic role: Sees Taffy slipping through with something valuable and makes a quiet choice to keep eating. A possible humane beat during the tension.

C6 — The Scrap Sweeper
Silhouette: Wiry humanoid towing a low bundle of metal with a long magnetic collecting pole.
Setup / motivation: Paid by recovered weight; hates useful fragments being trampled into the drain.
Quirk: Taps each piece against the pole and listens before adding it.
Choreographic role: The pole sweeps across Taffy's path; a metal click from the stolen device could expose him. Creates a small precise obstacle rather than another chase.
Useful alternate: A3

SERVICE ENTRANCE AND RELAY HALL

D1 — The Shift Marshal
Silhouette: Tall straight-backed human, faded high collar, broad freight-signalling paddle.
Setup / motivation: Wants the final scheduled load through before shift change.
Quirk: Keeps counting freight movements while dealing with people.
Choreographic role: Checks the door's authorization signal and allows the apparent maintenance traffic onward; the wanted broadcast then changes how they read the children.
Useful alternate: D2

D2 — The Pass Clerk
Silhouette: Round-helmeted humanoid at a small window, flat permit boards stacked like roof tiles.
Setup / motivation: Wants an impeccable handover so nobody can blame them for an access error.
Quirk: Aligns every battered permit edge before putting it down.
Choreographic role: Adds a human inspection after the key opens the electronic boundary. A raised pen or held stare creates uncertainty without demanding another stolen object.
Useful alternate: D1

D3 — The Relay Electrician
Silhouette: Wiry older human, short utility cape, loops of spare cable over one arm.
Setup / motivation: Trying to get a failing public display back into service and get paid.
Quirk: Tests it with the same two-finger tap after every repair.
Choreographic role: Can bring the screen back at exactly the wrong moment for the children: the successful repair reveals their wanted broadcast. Establish the repair before the reveal.
Useful alternate: A4

D4 — The Freight Counter
Silhouette: Broad-limbed humanoid with a little tally paddle held against one forearm.
Setup / motivation: Answers for every load moving through the hall; unexplained numbers mean unpaid work.
Quirk: Marks a tally with a sharp tap against their boot.
Choreographic role: Provides a readable flow of authorized movement to join or wait behind. Notices that the two small figures do not belong to the expected load.

D5 — The Hall Cleaner
Silhouette: Stooped human behind an extremely wide broom and a narrow wheeled bucket.
Setup / motivation: Needs one clean stretch of floor before the next freight movement ruins it.
Quirk: Turns the bucket to face the direction of the next sweep.
Choreographic role: Occupies the first convenient hiding place inside. Avoiding the fresh floor makes the children pause in the screen's sightline.
Useful alternate: D4

D6 — The Port Messenger
Silhouette: Slender offworld humanoid, long calves, one upright rigid delivery tube.
Setup / motivation: Needs to reach cargo dispatch before its next transfer departs.
Quirk: Always chooses their next doorway before finishing the current one.
Choreographic role: Their purposeful exit toward the cargo ramp plants the onward route to the port. When the alert appears, a stopped step and turned head raise the stakes.

FIRST SHORTLIST PROPOSAL
A1 Shade Stitcher, A2 Water Porter, B1 Doorpost, B2 The Tester, C1 Pipe Fitter,
D1 Shift Marshal. Six supporting players plus the existing core, spread across
four locations; enough separate tasks and sightlines for an initial blocking pass.
This is a suggested ensemble, not a selection made on the user's behalf.

Possible swaps:
- B5 Stillwater for B1 Doorpost: more surveillance tension, less overt pursuit.
- B3 Tally for B2 Tester: accounts/property discovery rather than a jewel-expert duel;
  also offers an origin for the wanted alert. Could be a tiny additional role if needed.
- C2 Cloth Carrier for A1 Shade Stitcher: moving cover rather than a fixed shade panel.
- C4 Pressure Warden for C1 Pipe Fitter: timed gate closure rather than sound cover.
- D2 Pass Clerk for D1 Shift Marshal: procedural discomfort rather than freight pressure.
- D3 Relay Electrician: optional replacement for a generic hall worker, giving the screen
  reveal a visible cause. One repair action should be planted before it pays off.
- C5 Late Lunch: optional quiet act of sympathy if a warmer service-court beat is wanted.

ONE POSSIBLE ACTION CHAIN (not a locked edit)
Market: children observe A1's cloth cycle and A2's awkward turn before splitting.
Forecourt: B1 holds the public boundary, B2 tries to inspect the jewel, broker leans
into Harper's act; Taffy takes the key. The shoulder lookout remains a distinct threat.
Withdrawal: Harper draws B1 through the public route and uses A1's cloth as a planned
screen. A2's valuable delivery is a physical delay the pursuer must navigate.
Service court: C1's pressure routine both helps and obstructs the separate approaches.
The children choose their moment and reunite at the blue pipe; nobody hands them
an effortless rescue or a second necessary key.
Relay: the stolen key authenticates the entrance; D1's freight schedule creates a
human timing problem. The inside broadcast turns routine scrutiny into recognition.
Stop at that reveal. Any later chase or port access is for the next planning pass.
