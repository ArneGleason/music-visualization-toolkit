## Pacing decisions

- There is only one frame (I24) between L23 and L24. The transport-yard approach therefore begins under L24, not in a separate instrumental establishing sequence. Use the relay exit as the geographic bridge.
- L26 is only 1.46 seconds. Start the scrapper exchange there and complete it during I27 (5.04 seconds). His sympathy is conveyed by his choice to conceal them; a possible orphan backstory stays subtext.
- The repair rhythm from L31 through L41 is fast. Keep the alignment and tapping as continuous actions across several lines. The lost component should be readable, not a tiny background detail.
- I42 lasts 23.21 seconds. Spend it on a donor-part errand, evasion and the compromised workaround, rather than extending repetitive cockpit repair.
- L48–L56 provides about 22 seconds for the sabotage. Use two linked service terminals and one simple repeating symbol/pulse pattern. The clearance crew unknowingly triggers the prepared interruption.
- L57 starts the lift. I58 gives 29.38 seconds for ascent, hope, failure, crash and cave discovery. Reserve roughly the last ten seconds for survival, scale and the ominous response; do not add a cave chase.

## Proposed causal chain

The original cartridge is lost → a visibly mismatched substitute gets the ship running → the scrapper is detained as clearance begins → unfinished repairs must suffice → Harper links the clearance start command to a temporary interruption of port controls → the clearance crew triggers their escape window → the substitute fails under sustained climb → a hard landing breaks through old mine ground → their presence wakes something below.

These details are proposals to make the images legible: the cartridge/adapter design, two-terminal arrangement, and dormant cave mechanism are not yet canon. The stolen relay credential is not silently repurposed as a universal port or ship key.

## Designs needed before starter images

- **Private yard security:** two recurring visible-faced guards, different heights/builds and gear arrangements; shared plain work-security insignia and practical partial helmets. They run a privately owned facility, not an anonymous military force. Keep them distinct from the water boss’s henchmen.
- **Scrapper:** a new sympathetic supporting character, recognizable through silhouette, working clothes and repair tools. Show wary generosity; no need to establish his childhood onscreen.
- **Ship:** a neglected local utility craft capable of attempting departure, with a patched pressure cabin and a plausible non-rotor lift/drive system. Avoid conventional helicopter silhouette. Lock exterior, interior, doorway, repair bay and size before generating multiple views.
- **Mechanical continuity:** original cartridge, incompatible substitute, adapter, closing linkage and crushed cartridge, warning lamp and failure location. The audience should recognize the same weak link during ascent.
- **Transport yard:** relay-side approach, checkpoint, concealment panel, scrapper corner, donor wreck, two control terminals, clearance machinery and takeoff route on one simple geography sheet.
- **Mine/cave:** reuse opening settlement and mining landmarks; place a covered old working beneath the crash site. Design cavern scale and one ambiguous awakening motif, leaving the next adventure open.

## Refinement priorities

1. Approve the emotional/story sequence and the scene boundaries before detailed shot prompts.
2. Barter item is now the fake jewel: the scrapper knows its significance for his collection. Decide how clearly his detention should read. Current proposal shows temporary removal, not a grim prison subplot.
3. Check the compact bargain and the final crash/cave sequence at song speed. If the ending is crowded, simplify the flight coverage rather than steal the cave reveal.
4. Resolve the ship silhouette and mechanical cause/effect before stills. Opening and return altitude should match visually; do not imply orbit or invent a new planet view.
5. After approval, merge selected shots into shots/shotlist.json, preserve existing authored work and use 24-frame in/out generation handles. These handles do not extend the musical edit. Use cuts for continuous action and consider short crossfades for travel/work time omitted; do not dissolve away the critical part loss or sabotage trigger.
