Input image role: visual reference ONLY for illustrated rendering, material language, warm sandstone/blue metal/copper palette and desert-colony world, NOT composition or characters. Create a different connected location. The reference's characters and pet MUST NOT appear.

Primary setting: a secondary controlled maintenance entrance into a freight/utility RELAY HALL in the same scavenger desert city, a next destination reached with a stolen asymmetrical horseshoe access key, not the ship or launch pad itself.
Composition: landscape 16:9, staging-friendly eye-level view from a shaded alley threshold looking diagonally through a partially OPEN heavy segmented industrial door into the hall. Make the doorway convincingly wide enough for two children to pass, with clear physical thickness, track, hinge/slide mechanism and a full clear walk-through opening. No open side passages that bypass this secured entrance.

Sun-warmed exterior edges contrast with cooler controlled hall inside. On the OUTSIDE jamb at a twelve-year-old child's comfortable reach, a small distinctive asymmetric horseshoe-shaped bronze/ivory physical reader or socket with one longer tine/slot and one shorter, and one restrained blue status light. A narrow amber lintel lamp and THREE stacked rectangular service vents in adjacent sandstone are useful repeatable landmarks.

INTERIOR AND REVEAL: through the opening, on a clearly interior far wall, place one LARGE rectangular flat dark-teal broadcast screen, about person-wide or larger, with a subtle thin warm amber frame/status edge. The screen is dark and blank, no faces, symbols, lettering or diagrams, reserved for a later exact-character wanted-broadcast overlay. It must be visible from just inside the entrance and read as an indoor public information screen, discovered AFTER entry. Do not mount it on the exterior facade. Keep it large, clean and unoccluded so its content can be composited later.
A small practical freight relay hall: old sorting benches, rail track or cargo-guide strip embedded in worn floor, warm bare masonry, chipped blue service doors, old pipes linked to selective newer ceramic sensor modules. Not neon cyberpunk, not a huge spaceship bridge, not a palace or high-tech showroom. A clear exit ramp recedes farther east/right toward utility gantries, and a narrow high daylight opening offers a distant cargo-crane silhouette to orient the journey toward the port. Tools and crates stay to the edges, no blockage of walking route. The hall is compact, believable and physically buildable, with clear separation of outside waiting patch, secured threshold, and inside screen reveal.

Style/medium: polished painted graphic-novel illustration matching supplied reference, controlled ink contours, naturalistic perspective and scale, fine crosshatching, richly observed tactile worn materials, restrained golden sand/ochre, chipped indigo-blue metal, cool teal screen, copper pipe highlights. Soft cool interior bounce and sharp warm sunlight on outer stone. Cinematic readable architecture for later character staging, not a map or top-down diagram.
Strict constraints: NO humanoids, people, children, aliens, pets, creatures or human silhouettes anywhere. No portraits or faces on the screen.
