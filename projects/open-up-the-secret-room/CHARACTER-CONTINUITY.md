## Starting-still review gate

1. Attach the fixed style anchor and the references for every visible character
   to each generation/edit. Label each reference's role. Keep any common prompt
   lock block identical across shots; place shot-specific action outside it.
2. Inspect the full composition for the same illustrative hand: contour weight,
   face simplification, eye rendering, shadow edges, texture/detail density and
   material highlights. Matching warm/teal colors alone is not a style pass.
   Underground lighting may change color and contrast without changing drawing.
3. Compare heads at similar displayed sizes beside their fixed anchors. Check
   Harper's hair endpoints and silhouette explicitly, then face geometry and
   age; check Taffy's face width, ears, curls and cheek marks. For the Scrapper,
   check beak/head shape, antennae and limb count. Check full-body proportions,
   costume and accessories separately. Occluded features are not verifiable.
4. Compare neighboring starters together for haircut, proportions, relative
   scale and drawing-style jumps. Always also compare to the fixed anchors.
5. Record shot ID, candidate path/version, reference versions, reviewer/date,
   separate identity/style/costume/anatomy findings and pass/revise/unverifiable
   status. Any visible identity or style mismatch requires revision. A hidden
   feature needs a better view where relevant, not an invented pass.
6. Correct the smallest affected area, preserving approved staging and faces
   elsewhere; review the edited result again. Show the user starting stills
   with references and unresolved issues before selecting them for animation.
7. Review the storyboard animatic before paid footage. After animation, inspect
   the start, middle, end and major head turns for new drift; a good starter
   does not guarantee stable motion identity. Record remaining motion issues.
