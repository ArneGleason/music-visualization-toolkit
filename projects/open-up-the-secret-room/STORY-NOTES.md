## Co-inventive symbiosis

Two technological species jointly invent this civilization. Both have language,
intelligence and agency. The ant-like people manipulate raw materials and build
precise geometric structures. Their partner is **distributed living infrastructure**:
a connected intelligence whose bodies constitute the buildings and systems.
Think of slime mold's ability to grow and fit a space, with much more varied
structural forms, membranes and moving luminous elements. It grows and adapts
in place; it does not walk around as separate creature-shaped agents.

The living species constructs through its own bodies and language. Individuality
and network boundaries remain open: neither a single all-controlling mind nor
many independent mobile workers is established. Ant-made components and living
structures can respond and adapt to one another as collaborators. Their touch
or signaling must not automatically imply that one species merely commands the
other. Neither servitude nor an ant-engineered origin is assumed.

Visual contrast: ant technology uses hard-edged geometry, repeated dimensions,
industrial materials and functional joints. Living infrastructure is organically
varied, locally luminous, and continuously integrated through walls, columns,
ceilings and channels. More science fiction, less fantasy ornament or gilded
filigree. Beautiful and strange, without exposed flesh or body horror. Images18
and19's ambulatory petal creatures are superseded concepts, not species canon.

Early waking machinery should now reveal the living building changing state:
light propagates, membranes unfold, channels or apertures change shape. Cargo
may move through this network, but is not assumed to be a separate creature.
The current text animatic v002 predates this visual refinement; incorporate it
when individual shots are designed.

## Orbital display and launch design

The destination display becomes simpler and less mechanical. Bodies may be
suspended by fields or represented virtually above a geometric platform. The
planet and both moons are **complete spheres**. Any crescent is illumination
on a sphere with a curved terminator, never a crescent-shaped object. The display
may emulate lighting with active surface shading, making its dark hemispheres
much darker than ambient illumination would ordinarily allow. Preserve a faint
whole-sphere silhouette so the design remains understandable. Exact display
technology is speculative; avoid complex mechanical rigging that would drift
or fail to make sense in animation.

The pod is boarded directly through launch-tube access. Show its interior and
ant-adapted controls/restraints; withhold the external craft until launch.
The pod has THREE matching ant stations arranged along a spherical inner shell,
facing inward around a narrow shared footwell. Keep the cabin snug, with only
enough clearance to board awkwardly; no fourth station. Walls and floor carry
layered equipment and connected living structures rather than large blank plates. These are not two custom human seats. Each
has a modest perch so Harper and Taffy can sit awkwardly while one station
remains unused. Ant ergonomics accommodate four arms, two legs and abdominal clearance.
The pod should feel like a coherent, precise high-tech vehicle, distinct from
surrounding rock architecture. Whether the craft is manufactured, living, or

## Working arc

Harper and Taffy want to get off-world. Their crash exposes an entrance to an
old underground civilization whose survival depends on remaining undiscovered.
The exterior statue recalls the Scrapper they met at the spaceport: someone
who seemed unique belongs to an entire hidden people.

They enter, witness the concealment illusion restoring the outside appearance
of unbroken rock, and continue through apparently deserted routes. The illusion
blocks light and hides the entrance; it does not need to be a physical barrier.
They are not going to escape by retracing their steps.

They discover an inhabited world, are cornered and captured, and are refused
release because they know its location. A chance to disable concealment offers
an apparent way to attract outside help. They understand that doing so would
expose the people below, and voluntarily leave the control alone.

One inhabitant witnesses this without their knowledge. The authorities remain
opposed to release, but the witness secretly frees them and guides them to an
escape pod. The helper risks discovery through the launch. Harper and Taffy
reach orbit using the civilization's technology while keeping its secret.

## What the audience needs to understand

- The statue, the Scrapper and the inhabitants belong to the same people.
- An exterior breach with a recognizable wreck becomes an apparently ordinary
  rock face. Repeating one camera setup makes the illusion legible.
- The inhabitants actively avoid outside observation. Show behavior and cause
  and effect rather than explaining a technical concealment system.
- The pair have a real opportunity, understand its cost to others, and choose
  to surrender it. Give the choice enough time to read before revealing the witness.
- The helper acts against the prevailing decision. Personal trust does not
  resolve the wider society's concern about discovery.
- Escape has a personal cost for the helper and achieves the pair's original
  off-world goal. It need not establish the helper's eventual punishment.

There is no explanatory dialogue or readable interface exposition. The music
and lyrics accompany a story told through action, composition, light and reaction.
