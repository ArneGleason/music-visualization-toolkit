# We Be Pop — the worlds

Every world here is named by the lyric. Nothing was invented to fit a concept;
the song already says where it is.

| section | world | the line that names it |
|---|---|---|
| intro | **RECORD** | — the needle drops |
| verse-1 | **MACHINE** | "I'd barely nudge the needle / on the arms that paint the signs" |
| chorus-1 | **RECORD** | "Sit square in the groove" |
| verse-2 | **OCEAN** | "Begin a thing beneath the waves / an ocean all around you" |
| chorus-2 | **RECORD** | "You're really on a groove" |
| verse-3 | **LINES** | "There's lots of little lines… but then I swap them more around" |
| recap | **MACHINE ⇄ OCEAN** | the lyric alternates, line by line — see below |
| turn | **OLD GAME** | "Remember to play the old game / a better old game" |
| unravel | **COMING APART** | "Was relatively is a was / is relatively was an is" |
| final-chorus | **RECORD** | everything returns, carrying debris |

## RECORD — home

The disc as geography. Radius is time: the stylus tracks from the outer edge
to the label across exactly 214.65 seconds, and the sections are visible bands
pressed into the surface, so you can always see how much song is left. She runs
the groove ahead of the needle. This is the chorus world because the chorus
says so — *sit square in the groove*.

## MACHINE — verse 1

Not the disc: the mechanism. Extreme close on the cartridge, the arm, the
stylus ploughing its furrow. "The arms that paint the signs / that draw the
rolling of the lines" is a description of a stylus tracking a groove, so shoot
it that way — macro, hot, industrial. "The heat beats down, and down, and out."

The verse ends on **"So big the world is all around us"** — that is the cue for
the widest shot in the video. Cut from a millimetre of groove to the entire
disc in one frame, on that line. It is the only place the film should breathe
out that hard, and the song asks for it.

## OCEAN — verse 2

It descends, and the lyric descends with it: "the colors that paint the floor /
the rolling rumbles under foot" (still on a surface) → "begin a thing beneath
the waves" (entering) → "get colder the lower you swim down" (descending) →
"an ocean all around you" (arrived). Four beats of a journey in four lines.
Blue-green going black, marine snow, shafts from a surface receding above.

The seabed should be grooved. It is still a record; she just cannot see the
edges any more.

## RECAP — the section that edits itself

Look at the order of the lines:

```
There's no way I'd take the dive.        ← verse 1
Begin a thing beneath the waves.         ← verse 2
The heat beats down, and down, and out.  ← verse 1
Get colder the lower you swim down.      ← verse 2
So big the world is all around us.       ← verse 1
An ocean all around you.                 ← verse 2
```

It **alternates**. The song is already cutting between the two verse worlds,
one line at a time. So the edit cuts on every lyric line and flips MACHINE ⇄
OCEAN with it — 12 cuts in 36 seconds, driven by the lyric, not by a rule.
This is the most structurally interesting section in the piece and it required
no invention at all.

## LINES — verse 3

"There's lots of little lines / I work all out… make sense in just one sort'a
order / but then I swap them more around." A diagram world. Glowing segments
assemble into an order and are then visibly reshuffled — on the MIDI notes,
which is where the Instrument Layer's 120 notes in this section live. The only
world where the type is architecture rather than overlay.

## OLD GAME — the turn

After `< hit jump into new section >` at bar 92.4, where the drums throw to the
Raum and the reverb decay automation opens. "Remember to play the old game /
brand new / a better old game." A fully 3D wilderness RPG level from around
2010: chunky geometry, baked lightmaps, dated bloom and a tiled grid that still
behaves like a board. Quirky forest adventurers and creature companions inhabit
it. The hardest tonal break in the video, and it lands on the hardest musical
one.

## COMING APART — unravel

Tenses collapse in the lyric, so the world loses its rules. The spiral stops
being concentric. Bands lose their edges. Geometry from the earlier worlds
bleeds through where it should not be. Eleven seconds only — it should feel
like a fault, not a destination.

## FINAL CHORUS — return

The record, but carrying debris from everywhere it has been. "Now not what it
was" arrives *inside* the chorus — the unravel line surviving into the return.
End at the run-out groove.
